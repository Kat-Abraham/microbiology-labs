Why do this lab?

Microorganisms differ in how they use or tolerate oxygen. Some require it for growth; others are harmed by it. These differences help microbiologists interpret cultures and choose appropriate growing conditions.

How it works: Fluid thioglycollate medium contains a reducing agent that lowers oxygen availability. Oxygen diffuses in from the surface, so it is most abundant near the top and least abundant near the bottom.

The pink indicator (resazurin) shows where oxygen is present near the surface; it does not show bacterial growth.

After incubation, the location and density of visible growth reveal the organism's oxygen relationship. In this model, darker dots represent growth.

This is a simplified simulation using fictional unknown cultures—not a real culture or a diagnostic test. There is nothing to submit. Learn all five patterns for the exam.

Review: five growth patterns

  • Obligate aerobe: growth at the top; requires oxygen.
  • Obligate anaerobe: growth at the bottom; oxygen inhibits growth.
  • Facultative anaerobe: growth throughout but heavier near the top; uses oxygen when available but can grow without it.
  • Aerotolerant anaerobe: fairly even growth throughout; tolerates oxygen but does not use it for aerobic respiration.
  • Microaerophile: a band slightly below the surface; requires oxygen at concentrations lower than atmospheric levels.

Self-check

Why is the broth pink near the top?

Resazurin turns pink when oxidized, indicating oxygen near the surface. The color does not itself represent bacterial growth.

Why does a facultative anaerobe grow throughout the tube but more densely at the top?

It can grow with or without oxygen, but aerobic respiration often supports more growth when oxygen is available.

How do you distinguish aerotolerant from facultative anaerobes?

Aerotolerant anaerobes typically grow evenly throughout; facultative anaerobes typically show denser growth near the top.

Why do microaerophiles form a band below the surface?

They need oxygen, but the concentration at the surface is too high for optimal growth.
