Introduction: Why and how this test works

Microorganisms differ in their ability to use or tolerate oxygen. Some need oxygen for aerobic respiration; others are harmed by it or grow without using it. Oxygen can also generate reactive oxygen species that damage cells unless cells have protective systems.

Fluid thioglycollate medium contains nutrients and reducing agents, including thioglycollate, that help maintain a gradient: oxygen is most available near the air-exposed surface and least available deeper in the tube. Some formulations contain a small amount of agar to slow oxygen movement and an indicator such as resazurin (pink when oxidized). After a culture grows, the location and density of the cells provide evidence of its oxygen requirement.

Why this matters: knowing a microorganism’s oxygen needs helps laboratories choose appropriate cultivation conditions and helps explain where microbes can survive in the environment or body.

Interpretation guide

Obligate aerobe: growth at top only.
Obligate anaerobe: growth at bottom only.
Facultative anaerobe: growth throughout, heaviest at top.
Aerotolerant anaerobe: growth distributed relatively evenly.
Microaerophile: a narrow band just below the surface.

Check your understanding

Think about your answer first, then open each explanation. These concepts may appear on your exam.

1. How does fluid thioglycollate medium produce an oxygen gradient?

Oxygen diffuses in from the air at the surface. Reducing agents such as thioglycollate remove dissolved oxygen deeper in the tube, and a small amount of agar in some formulations slows mixing. This leaves higher oxygen near the top and little or none toward the bottom.

2. How do facultative anaerobes differ from aerotolerant anaerobes?

Both can grow throughout the tube. Facultative anaerobes generally grow more densely at the top because they can use oxygen for aerobic respiration. Aerotolerant anaerobes tolerate oxygen but do not use it for aerobic respiration, so their growth is relatively uniform.

3. Why does a microaerophile grow just below the surface?

Microaerophiles require oxygen, but at concentrations lower than those in the air-exposed top layer. They form a band where the oxygen concentration is suitable.

4. Why is oxygen harmful to some bacteria?

Oxygen metabolism and exposure can generate reactive oxygen species, such as superoxide and hydrogen peroxide. Cells without adequate protective enzymes or other defenses can be damaged by these molecules.
